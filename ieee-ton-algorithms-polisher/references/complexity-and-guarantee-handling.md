# Complexity and Guarantee Handling

Use this reference when the draft discusses runtime complexity, convergence, optimality, approximation, feasibility, or stability.

## Complexity

- Preserve the user's stated order unless it is clearly a typo.
- Express complexity in terms of the paper's own dimensions, such as numbers of users, servers, tasks, antennas, subchannels, vehicles, UAVs, time slots, or iterations.
- If nested loops are described but no complexity is stated, a cautious summary may say that the cost scales with the number of outer iterations and the cost of solving each subproblem. Do not invent a closed-form order without enough detail.

## Convergence and guarantees

- Do not claim global optimality unless the draft proves it.
- Do not claim convergence to a stationary point unless the required assumptions and argument are present.
- If the method is heuristic, describe it as a heuristic or practical algorithm rather than a guaranteed optimizer.
- If the draft contains a proof sketch, polish the logical sequence without strengthening the theorem.

## Safe wording

- `Under the stated assumptions, the sequence generated by the algorithm ... .`
- `The complexity is dominated by ... .`
- `The algorithm provides a feasible solution by ... .`
- `The method is used to obtain a high-quality solution for the original problem.`

Avoid unsafe wording such as `guarantees the optimal solution`, `always converges`, or `has low complexity` unless supported.
